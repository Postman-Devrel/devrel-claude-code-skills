# What makes a great hackathon

Distilled from published organizer guides, research on project continuation, and judge and participant write-ups. Use these to challenge answers during the interview and to shape the brief. Sources are at the bottom.

## Principles, and the interview question each one drives

| # | Principle | Test during the interview | If it fails |
|---|-----------|---------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Define the goal first.** Each goal needs a different format, audience, and prize structure. Most failures trace back to decisions made before the event. | Does every goal have a number, a source, and a date? | Convert "awareness" and "engagement" into counts, or drop them. |
| 2 | **Specific problem and theme.** A challenge that could belong to any company attracts nobody who cares about the product. | Could this theme be run by a competitor unchanged? | Tie it to a real workflow: a named API, an agent that must reach production, a verifiable badge path. |
| 3 | **Publish judging criteria before registration opens.** Use 4 to 5 weighted criteria tied to the program goal. Vague criteria drive off serious builders. | Are criteria and weights in the brief and on the Luma page? | Add a "Judging" section to the registration page task. |
| 4 | **Independent judges.** Do not let sponsors judge alone. Brief and calibrate judges by scoring a sample project together. | Is there at least one judge with no stake in either company? | Add an independent judge and a 20 minute calibration. |
| 5 | **Remove setup friction before the event.** Credits, API keys, sandboxes, and workspaces ready at kickoff. Early friction loses the best participants. | Who provisions access, and is it tested with a fresh account? | Add a dry run with a new user the week before. |
| 6 | **Onboard early.** Starter kit, sample repo, and setup guide one week out reduce no-shows and raise submission quality. | Does a starter kit exist, and who owns it? | Add as a task with an owner and date. |
| 7 | **Registration is a quality filter and a team tool.** Capture role, skills, and company; use it to form teams and match target accounts. | What do we capture at signup? | Add the fields and a team-formation channel. |
| 8 | **Keep everything in one place.** Registration, submissions, comms, and support in as few tools as possible (Luma, one Discord channel, one submission form). | How many places must a participant visit? | Consolidate. |
| 9 | **Mentors are the product.** Real engineers, visible and reachable. Plan about one per 8 to 10 attendees. Mentors from partner companies drift toward their own product; brief them to help with the participant's goal. | Who is staffed, and are they briefed? | Add a mentor brief and a schedule. |
| 10 | **Support first-timers and mixed skill levels.** Buddy pairing, signage, a help desk. | Is the audience mixed? | Add a help desk and a beginner-friendly challenge. |
| 11 | **Working beats ambitious.** Judges reward a working demo with fewer features over a broken one with many. Interface and demo quality drive scores. | Does the rubric reward a working demo? | Add a working-demo gate to the submission rules and a 3 minute demo format. |
| 12 | **Prizes match the audience.** Cash alone is weak for senior engineers. Funding, credits, access, or a path to production motivates them more, and matches enterprise buyers. | Does the prize fit the persona? | Add a continuation prize (credits, funded project, exec sponsor). |
| 13 | **Run the logistics tests.** Test Wi-Fi, power, projectors, and the submission flow the day before. Plan capacity above target because registrants do not all attend. Food drives morale. | Has someone owned the venue test? | Add a day-before checklist. |
| 14 | **Follow up within 48 hours.** Publish repos and a public recap, and send personal notes to standout teams, including non-winners. Strong non-winners who disappear are the most expensive loss. | Is there a 48 hour plan? | Add the follow-up tasks with owners. |
| 15 | **Plan for continuation.** Most prototypes are abandoned. Continuation depends on funding, skill mix on the team, and integration into real work. Intensive short sprints alone lower the odds of long-term continuation. | What happens to the top 5 projects at day 30? | Add a continuation path: funding, a community listing, a sales or SE owner. |
| 16 | **Measure at 30, 60, and 90 days.** Count teams still building, tools adopted, deals influenced, not just attendance. | Are follow-up measurement dates in the brief? | Add dates and owners. |
| 17 | **Build an alumni community.** A Discord channel that stays active between events. Treat each hackathon as the start of a relationship. | Where do participants go afterward? | Add a channel and a monthly touchpoint. |
| 18 | **Be transparent and fair.** Public rules, public results, feedback to teams, and a demo showcase. Hidden judging damages credibility for years. | Are rules, scoring, and results public? | Add a results post and feedback task. |

## How to use these

- Raise at most three principle conflicts per round, the ones with the highest impact on the user's stated goals. Say the principle in plain words and offer one fix.
- In the brief, record each unresolved conflict under "Risks and open questions" instead of silently accepting it.
- Closed and joint customer events weigh principles 5, 12, 14, and 15 most. Public events weigh 3, 7, 8, 13, and 17 most. Challenge-based events weigh 3, 11, and 18 most because verification must be unambiguous.

## Sources

- [AngelHack: Hackathon best practices](https://angelhack.com/blog/hackathon-best-practices/): goal first, KPIs beyond headcount, infrastructure ready, early onboarding, published criteria, blind scoring and judge calibration, 48 hour follow-up, 30/60/90 day measurement, alumni community.
- [Unstop: 10 red flags hackathons will not attract top talent](https://unstop.com/blog/red-flags-hackathon-wont-attract-top-talent): generic problem statements, vague judging, misaligned prizes, poor participant experience, no follow-up system.
- [Frank Wang: What I learned from judging a hackathon](https://medium.com/@ffwang2/what-i-learned-from-judging-a-hackathon-8545e871cceb): working product over feature count, demo and interface quality, independent judges.
- [Nordic APIs: How to hold a killer first hackathon](https://nordicapis.com/how-to-hold-a-killer-first-hackathon-or-developer-conference/): reliable network and capacity buffer, food and morale, prizes that match effort.
- [Research on post-hackathon project continuation](https://wiredspace.wits.ac.za/items/6f98f71b-06df-4ba6-bf10-167300195093): funding, team skill diversity, and integration into operations drive continuation.
- [A model of project continuation in game jams and hackathons](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu/items/26ad7881-2141-4698-9229-afdb08ecb730): short-term versus long-term continuation factors.
- [Mattermost: how to run a hackathon](https://handbook.mattermost.com/contributors/guidelines/how-to-run-a-hackathon) and the [AngelHack checklist](https://angelhack.com/wp-content/uploads/Hackathon-Best-Practices-Checklist.docx.pdf): checklist-style planning, define the why first.
